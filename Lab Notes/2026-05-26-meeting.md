## Main Things To Do

### 1. Understand the Physics of the Toy Motor

We need to figure out how the Hexbug moves.

Important questions:

- How does the motor create motion?
- Why does the toy move forward?
- Why does the toy move in a circular path near a surface?
- Does the motor direction affect the turning direction?
- Does the weight distribution affect the turning direction?

Simple explanation:

A Hexbug moves because its internal motor vibrates the body. The angled legs interact with the ground through friction, and this converts vibration into forward motion.

---

### 2. Compare Hexbug Motion to Bacterial Motion

We need to understand why bacteria can also move in circular paths near surfaces.

Important questions:

- Why do bacteria move in circles near surfaces?
- Is the Hexbug’s circular motion caused by the same physics as bacteria?
- What are the similarities and differences between Hexbug motion and bacterial motion?

Important caution:

Even if the Hexbug and bacteria both move in circles, that does not automatically mean the mechanism is the same. Bacteria use flagella and fluid dynamics, while Hexbugs use vibration, legs, and friction.

---

### 3. Make the Prototype Toy Neutrally Buoyant

One goal is to modify the toy so that it becomes **neutrally buoyant**.

Simple meaning:

A neutrally buoyant object does not sink or float strongly. It stays suspended in the fluid.

Questions to think about:

- How much weight should be added or removed?
- Does waterproofing change the buoyancy?
- Will the Hexbug still move if it is not strongly touching the bottom surface?
- Will the motion change in mineral oil or other fluids?

---
