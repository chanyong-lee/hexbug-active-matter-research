## Density of Float Materials

## Objective

The purpose of this lab note was to calculate the density of possible float materials for the neutral buoyancy Hexbug experiment.

We tested two materials:

1. Polyethylene foam
2. Cork

These density values will be used to calculate how much flotation material is needed to make the Hexbug neutrally buoyant in mineral oil.

---

## Foam Density Measurement

### Method

We measured the side lengths of the foam block and measured its mass using a scale.

The foam volume was calculated using:

> Volume = length × width × height

### Measurements

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Length | 5.0 cm |
| Width | 4.4 cm |
| Height | 1.8 cm |
| Volume | 39.6 ± 2.5 cm³ |
| Mass | 0.7 ± 0.1 g |

### Calculation

> Density = Mass / Volume

Using the measured values:

> Density = 0.7 g / 39.6 cm³

### Result

> Density of foam = 0.18 ± 0.026 g/cm³

This value is reasonable for premium polyethylene foam.

---

## Cork Density Measurement

### Method

The cork was treated as a cylinder.

We measured the radius and height of the cork, then calculated the volume using the cylinder volume formula:

> Volume = πr²h

After calculating the volume, we measured the mass and calculated density.

---

## Cork 1: Leonetti

### Measurements

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---:|
| Volume | 15.08 ± 2.5 cm³ |
| Mass | 3.0 ± 0.1 g |

### Calculation

> Density = Mass / Volume

Using the measured values:

> Density = 3.0 g / 15.08 cm³

### Result

> Density of cork = 0.199 ± 0.034 g/cm³

## Discussion

Both foam and cork have densities much lower than mineral oil.  
This means both materials can provide upward buoyant force when attached to the Hexbug.

The foam has a slightly lower density than the cork, so it should provide slightly more flotation for the same volume.

However, the final material choice should also consider:

- Ease of cutting
- Ease of attachment
- Shape control
- Symmetry
- Waterproofing
- Stability in mineral oil

Foam may be easier to cut into two equal pieces and attach symmetrically to the Hexbug.

---

## Connection to Neutral Buoyancy Calculation

These density values will be used in the neutral buoyancy equation:

> Float Volume = (Hexbug Mass - Mineral Oil Density × Hexbug Volume) / (Mineral Oil Density - Float Density)

Using these values, we can estimate how much foam or cork is needed to make the Hexbug neutrally buoyant in mineral oil.
